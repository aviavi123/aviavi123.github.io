OverScores

iPhone / iPad / Mac App

OverScores is a sheet music reader focused on the features musicians use most, without the complexity of a large suite of tools. It's designed to help you practice, learn, and memorize music.

Read, then memorize

OverScores lets you hide individual measures or staves while reading a piece, so you can gradually rely less on the page. You can hide sections of a piece, work through difficult passages, and test your recall as you practice.

Audio playback includes A/B looping and speed control, making it easy to slow down a passage while learning it. You can also play audio from Apple Music, YouTube, and Spotify alongside your music.

Clean up scanned music

OverScores can improve the appearance of poor-quality scans by straightening bowed or curved staves and removing smudges and gray backgrounds.

Measures can be detected automatically, and title, composer, and tempo markings fields can be automatically populated from scans. Large PDF compendiums can also be split into individual pieces.

Practice tools

  • Audio recording
  • Metronome with tap tempo and meter control
  • Practice time logging
  • Keyboard with high-quality piano samples, playable on-screen or via MIDI input
  • Bluetooth page-turn
  • Three markup layers with comprehensive annotation tools
  • Jumps for instantly flipping to a repeat or coda
  • MusicXML chord extraction and transposition
  • Side-by-side page view in landscape

Bring your existing library

If you already use ForScore or Newzik, you can import your setlists, including their markups, preserving setlist order.

Your music library and annotations can sync between iPad, iPhone, and Mac through iCloud, or be transferred using library export. One purchase works across your devices.

Try everything free for 14 days

OverScores includes a 14-day free trial with access to all features. There is no subscription, automatic billing, or email signup.

After the trial, some premium features are locked, but OverScores continues to work. You keep full access to everything you imported and every markup you made during the trial.

Importing pieces, Library and Setlists
Library / SetlistsLibrary contains all your pieces. Setlists are groups of pieces.
ImportYou can import a piece in several ways: from Files (PDFs, .oscore or MusicXML (chords only), ForScore and Newzik setlists), from your Photos library or using the Scan with camera. Scans that have a bowed (warped) appearance are auto-corrected, and imported files that have bowed staves can be straightened in a piece's Clarity menu. OverScores automatically adds the title, composer and tempo indication, when available - but you can change this in the information panel.
SearchSearch the Library by title or composer, and Setlists by setlist name or by the titles and composers inside them.
SortYou can sort the Library by tapping on the column header; you can sort pieces within a setlist via the up/down arrow.
SelectPress select to select and delete pieces from the Library.
Press and hold a rowA long press on a piece brings up a menu for changing a piece's information, adding it to a setlist, Sharing the piece or deleting it.
Piece and setlist countsThe right-hand end of the Library / Setlists bar shows either the total ("29 pieces", or "4 setlists"), or when searching, the number of hits. (Not shown on a narrow screen, since there is no room for it.)
Setlists

A setlist is pieces arranged in playing order. Pieces stay in the library — the same piece can sit in several setlists at once, and taking it out of a setlist never deletes it.

Making a setlist as you import

Choosing several files at once from Files asks Put these in a setlist? Name it and the setlist is made holding all of the imported pieces, in the order they were imported; answer Not now for the selected pieces to be added to the Library without going into a Setlist. This is only offered when more than one piece is imported at once.

Importing music from other apps

Aside from importing single files one at a time, you can export a whole setlist from another app and import it into OverScores.

From Newzik

Newzik exports a setlist as a zip holding a folder for each piece, and OverScores reads the whole archive in one go: the pieces land in the Library and a setlist named after the zip file is created.

In NewzikFrom Setlists, press and hold the desired setlist, choose Export to PDF, pick your export options, then Save to Files (or other location of your choosing).
In OverScoresImport from Files…, and choose the .zip Newzik saved.
The running orderThe zip does not record it, so the pieces arrive sorted by name. Open the setlist, tap Edit, and drag them into playing order.
From ForScore
In ForScoreFrom Setlists, press and hold the desired setlist, choose Share, then Include Scores — use the ⋯ to include or exclude annotations — then Save to Files (or another location of your choosing).
In OverScoresImport from Files…, and choose the .4ss setlist file. The imported setlist preserves the ForScore setlist order.
Markings made in another app

Annotations in a ForScore file that enabled Include extras are imported into OverScores as Layer 3 in the markup panel. They show up where you drew them. Turn Layer 3 off to read the music clean, or delete the marking on a page to be rid of it there. What they are not is ink — ForScore flattens them to a picture on the way out, so they can be shown, hidden and removed but not erased a stroke at a time, nor edited. However, the OverScores pen can annotate above the imported annotations.

With Newzik, anything exported as part of the page stays part of the page. An Annotated PDF arrives with its markings printed into the music: you can see them, but OverScores cannot move, recolour or erase them, nore hide them. Its own pen draws on a separate layer, which of course is editable.

Export the plain PDF instead if you would rather start with clean music and mark it up within OverScores.

Practice time

OverScores keeps a record of when you opened a piece of music. There is no stop or start — it counts while the music is open for longer than one minute. (Note: the microphone is not listening to whether you are actually playing a piece.) Practice time is shown in two places: as a Practice section at the bottom of a piece's Information, and as a Last practiced column you can sort the Library by.

SessionsHow many times a piece has been opened. Closing the piece, the screen timing out, or leaving the app altogether ends the session.
TotalThe total time spent with the piece open.
Last sessionHow long the most recent practice session lasted.
Chord charts from MusicXML

When importing a MusicXML file (.musicxml, .xml or .mxl), OverScores can identify chord names on a piece and create a chord chart with measures. An imported MusicXML chord chart can be transposed; a scanned PDF cannot.

What is free, and what OverScores Pro adds

After the trial expiration, the free version will continue to have whatever is in your library, along with all markups, etc. Of course, if you buy OverScores, the same libary you started will continue with everything intact.

A free library can increase in size by 25 more pieces and 10 more setlists than it had when the trial ended. So if you brought 100 pieces into OverScores during your trial, you keep all one hundred pieces and can add twenty-five more. Deleting something makes room again. You also retain markups, though limited to black pencil and yellow highlighter. All Settings options remain in the app too.

The Pro features are all indicated by a small padlock icon. Tapping on any of them gives you the option to purchase the full Pro version.
